# Schema 1.0: downloadable claims and events

Version 1.0.0 defines sourced institute records and downloadable events. Authored data contains primary facts and evidence bindings. `scripts/published.py:publish` materialises facts, resolves stable references, then derives field citations and display selections without mutating its input. The build validates its result against `schema/aisi.schema.json` before JSON, CSV, events or page output; the slide map uses the same producer.

## Authored and published contracts

The strict authored schema is derived from the download schema by `authored_schema` in `scripts/references.py`. It requires stable excerpt selectors, component IDs in funding `sum_of`, and explicit output `credit`. It forbids generated jurisdiction status and status dates, institute status and establishment dates, Known activity, first-output timeline events, exchange-rate crosses, USD annual estimates, compatibility `credited_from`, and all `citations` and `display` fields. The conditional funding fields generated from components, means or profiles are listed under [Generated funding values](#generated-funding-values). These values belong in downloads; editors change their primary inputs.

The build uses this strict boundary after the authored-data migration (`AUTHORING_STRICT`). Non-strict adapters accept legacy fixtures and migration inputs; they are not an alternative authored format for production. Publication first derives facts, then resolves stable selectors against the resulting arrays, then attaches field evidence and display choices. The published schema validates the complete result. A download is therefore not a valid strict authoring input. Migration order is stable references, reviewed credit assignments, then primary facts. The facts migration accepts an optional manifest of changed excerpt bindings for semantic review. It rejects automatic transfer of aggregate or conversion evidence to component inputs and removes obsolete comparisons against computed copies from the shared-claim registry.

## Agreements

`institutes[].agreements` records a `counterparty` and `counterparty_type`: `developer`, `institute`, `government`, `multilateral` or `other`. Its `state` is `signed`, `announced` or `pledged`; optional `date`, `scope` and `source_ids` describe the agreement and its evidence. Which agreements qualify is set by `dataset.method` (Agreements). `agreements.csv` exports these records.

## Identifiers and provenance

Jurisdictions have optional `iso3` (ISO 3166 alpha-3), `m49` (three-digit string, retaining leading zeroes) and `wikidata` (QID). Institutes have optional `wikidata`. Non-null identifiers require `identifier_source_ids`, separate from the sources for the status. `identifier_notes` records distinctions or search gaps. Null or omission means unknown or inapplicable; the notes distinguish these cases. The EU has no country-equivalent ISO3/M49 code. The tracked roster stays at 193 UN members, Taiwan and the EU; it does not expand to other observers or territories.

Each QID is checked against retrieved Wikidata labels and entity type or institutional parent/country, with the retrieval date and label in the source record and research evidence. A headquarters country is not necessarily the institutional jurisdiction. Builds check syntax, uniqueness across jurisdictions and institutes together, and citations, not the live contents of Wikidata.

Related bodies may carry their own `source_ids`. All newly researched AI Act authority records do so and explicitly set `counts_as_aisi: false`. A proposed designation is described as proposed. No entry in a Commission list is not proof of no national designation.

The authority pass adds optional `designation_state` (`designated` or `proposed`) and `authority_roles` (`market_surveillance`, `notifying`, `single_point_of_contact`). Either structured field requires the body's own sources and `counts_as_aisi: false`. Omission means unresolved or inapplicable, rather than no designation. Role text preserves sector limits and pending provisions; the structured fields do not turn a proposal into an enacted designation. Both fields are included in `related_bodies.csv`.

## Source excerpts

`sources[].excerpts` holds `{text, supports, gloss?, url?, accessed?}`. Text is verbatim in the original language, at most 300 characters; non-English excerpts need a short English `gloss`. Optional `url` and `accessed` identify the copy actually read. In authored data, `supports` is a non-empty array of stable selectors: property-name steps alternate with matches such as `{"id": "gb-aisi"}`. Output rows use `lineage`, rates use `currency`, and memberships use `body`; these natural keys must be updated together with their bindings if corrected. Other referenced array records have permanent IDs. In downloads, each selector becomes an RFC 6901 pointer from the root of that exact published JSON, never a URL fragment. A selector containing identity matches is not an RFC 6901 pointer.

The build resolves every pointer, checks JSON pointer escaping and array indices, and requires the nearest enclosing record with citations to cite the excerpt's source. Identifiers use `identifier_source_ids`; other claims use `source_ids`. Nested agreements, outputs, people, funding, names, timeline events and network milestones must have their own citations under the schema, so a parent citation cannot repair a missing or unrelated citation. A valid pointer establishes a citation link, not that the quoted words prove the claim; that remains an editorial check.

Download pointers are version-specific because JSON arrays have positional indices. Resolution happens after generated first-output events are inserted. Insertion and reordering therefore retain authored claim identity, including when neighbours share a source. Funding `sum_of` uses stable component IDs in authored data and downloads. Existing shared-claim target selectors remain stable in both forms; their coverage report resolves them to download pointers. Binding validation rejects missing or ambiguous identities. The migration preserves established IDs rather than regenerating them after content edits.

## Outputs, names and funding

`institutes[].outputs` holds `{date, type, title, lineage, attribution, qualifies_as, qualification_note, source_ids}`, with optional `attribution_note`, plus `credit: {institute_id, date}`. Publication date and institutional credit date have separate meanings and separate bindings, even when equal. The download compatibility field `credited_from` is generated when the credit date differs from the publication date. Types are `evaluation`, `joint_testing`, `report`, `tool`, `guidance`, `dataset` and `other`. Which items belong in the catalogue, and what to cite, is set by `dataset.method` (Outputs).

`names` holds the name history, oldest first, including documented predecessors. Each name links by `event` to the jurisdiction timeline event that gave it (a designation, a mandate change, a commitment naming the planned body, or a `named` event where no other act gives the name), and its `from` is generated from that event's date, never authored; evidence for the date binds to the event's `date`. The events export has no name rows: a name change appears once, as the timeline event the name links to. `mandate_changed` and `named` are timeline codes with no status effect. Status follows the primary acts described under [Timeline and status](#timeline-and-status). First output includes any publication in the broad catalogue, including guidance; it does not necessarily identify the first evaluation. A `partner_call` records recruitment of collaborating institutions without changing status.

Funding may have `decision_date`, distinct from its fiscal `period`, and a stated-currency annual figure with a required `annual_basis`. Published `annual_amount` is either a primary stated figure or generated from the inputs described under [Generated funding values](#generated-funding-values). The basis distinguishes an annual allocation from an arithmetic mean of a documented multi-year amount. Neither is actual spending. Ongoing future funding is labelled with its future period. No decision date is inferred from a fiscal period and no exchange rate is invented. People may have an `until` date to distinguish former appointments.

`dataset.exchange_rates` retains one table of ECB euro foreign exchange reference rates (annual averages, series `EXR.A.<currency>.EUR.SP00.A`), covering every currency used in funding except EUR, the base, with `currency`, published `units_per_eur`, derived `eur_per_unit`, `basis`, inclusive `period` and `source_ids`. `eur_per_unit` is 1 divided by `units_per_eur`, rounded half up to 12 significant figures. The cited source carries the URL and access date, and `exchange_rates.csv` repeats them as `source_urls` and `source_accessed`. The reference year is the calendar year of `dataset.as_of`; while that year is incomplete, the latest complete year supplies the rates. The build checks currency coverage and uniqueness, one shared period that is the reference calendar year, basis and source set, positive finite values, primary citations and the arithmetic. Excerpts cover the published inputs and period dates. The CSV flattens the dates to `period_start` and `period_end`; the datapackage describes each field.

## Head, reporting line and national partners

Three optional institute objects share one state pattern. `state` is `stated`, `not_published` or `not_applicable`. The schema holds the per-state field rules: `stated` carries its value and `source_ids` (partners carry theirs per entry), `not_published` needs `notes`, and the other states forbid the value. `not_published` means searched and nothing published; `notes` says what was searched, in which languages and when, and the build requires a year in it.

- `head`: `person_id` is the id of an entry of the institute's own `people`, so name and title are stated once. The build rejects an id that is not of this institute, or whose person has an `until`.
- `reports_to`: `name` (source language) and optional `name_en`; several bodies are joined with '; '. The rule is in `dataset.method` (Reporting line).
- `national_partners`: `entries`, as many as meet the partner rule, in authored order. Each has `name`, `name_en`, `acronym`, `what` (two to six words), `relation` (`member`, `works_with` or `planned`), optional `cyber_agency` (true only), `how` (one sentence of at most 25 words, written from the bound quote) and `source_ids`. The host is never an entry. `member` is reserved for an institute whose `legal_form` is `federation_of_existing_bodies`. A stated list without a `cyber_agency` entry needs `notes` naming the cyber agency searched and the date.

Relation words live only in `dataset.vocabularies.partner_relation` (`{label, definition}`) and the muted tail in `partner_cyber_none_published`. The card, the checks and the CSVs read them from there. The three relation codes fix card order (cyber agency first, then member, works_with, planned, then authored order).

Evidence: for a stated row the value leaves are bound by excerpts (`head/person_id`, `reports_to/name`, and each entry's `name`, `relation` and `how`). Each `what` descriptor and present `cyber_agency` also needs an exact binding, and an institute has at most one flagged entry. `STRICT_PARTNER_DETAILS` in `scripts/row_checks.py` enforces these checks in production; its non-strict setting supports legacy fixtures only. Descriptor and cyber-flag bindings contribute to the card citation group. Field citations are generated as for every other record. The fields are optional until the data lands, but once any institute has one every institute has all three.

Generated outputs: `institutes[].display.rows` (texts, partner order, relation words, the cyber-none-published tail and one citation list per row; partner `items` are capped at six, while `fold_items` carries every partner with `name`, `how` and their `cited` sources), `institutes.csv` columns `head_state`, `head_person_id`, `reports_to_state`, `reports_to`, `reports_to_en`, `national_partners_state`, `head_source_ids`, `reports_to_source_ids` and the three `_notes` search logs, and `national_partners.csv` with every entry. The per-record API under `api/v1/` carries the fields unchanged. The card shows three rows after Form in the order Head, Reports to, Partners: only a stated row has a value, not applicable is the muted dash, anything else reads Not published, and an institute without the fields shows no rows. The partners line is one wrapping line of "name (what, relation word)" items, "ANSSI (national cyber security agency, member) · …", with at most six items, a muted tail ("cyber agency none published") when no entry has `cyber_agency`, and one citation group at the end. A closed fold-out under the line, "How they work together", gives every partner's `how` sentence in the same priority order, with one citation group per partner, covering its name and how. The card citation group remains limited to the six partners on its line. Card items repeat the name/how `cited` list from their corresponding fold items; that list is not the card line's full evidence list.

Tests: `tests/test_rows.py` holds each gate check with a mutation; `check_leadership_rows` in `scripts/check_page.py` works the expected rows out from the data and the vocabularies, independently of the producer.

## Testing capacity and model access

Two institute objects, required on every institute, in the downloads and the API. The page shows them only in its Testing and access groups, cited from that section's own source list, and their sources stay off the card's source list (`DOWNLOAD_ONLY_FIELDS` in `scripts/display.py`), so a source cited only by them must not be added to the institute's own `source_ids`. The rule is in `dataset.method` (Testing capacity and model access); codes, labels and definitions are in `dataset.vocabularies.testing_capacity` and `dataset.vocabularies.model_access`. Checks are in `scripts/flag_checks.py`.

`testing_capacity` is either `modes`, one or both of `in_house` and `via_other_bodies`, each `{code, source_ids, named_as?}`, held once each in vocabulary order; or a `state`: `planned` (needs `source_ids`) or `not_published` (needs `searches`). Outside `planned`, top-level `source_ids` are the sources of the `notes` only and require them. Natural key of a mode: `code` (in `IDENTITY_KEYS` and in the standalone copy in `scripts/check.py`; a test keeps the two in agreement).

`model_access` records what sources state and generates its value:

- `access_events`: `{id, model, developer, date, kind, epoch_basis, epoch_model, epoch_source_id, source_ids, named_as?}`. `kind` is `pre_release` or `deeper_than_public`. `date` keeps the source's precision and is not after `as_of`. `epoch_basis` says why the event counts: `frontier_model` (`epoch_model` is the event's model in Epoch AI's frontier set) or `leading_developer`, which adds `epoch_model_date`, `epoch_notable_model` and `epoch_notable_date`: `epoch_model` is then a frontier model of the developer released on or after 2023-01-01 and by the event (its last possible day not after the event's), and the notable model's release falls in the twelve months before the event (from the same day a year before the event's first possible day to its last possible day). The authored event carries this basis and the build checks the dates; it does not recompute it from Epoch's data. Every Epoch field is bound to an excerpt from `epoch_source_id`, a source published by Epoch AI that is also among the event's `source_ids`.
- `legal_power`: `{act, provision, conditions, source_ids, named_as?}`. `provision` names the article; its excerpt comes from a primary source marked `legal_text` whose title or English title contains `act`, so an explainer cannot stand in for the legal text.
- `developer_agreements`: `{id, agreement_id, epoch_model, epoch_source_id, source_ids, named_as?}`. `agreement_id` is an entry of the institute's `agreements` with a developer counterparty and a date not after `as_of`, so date and counterparty are stated once; its excerpt says the agreement provides for access to, or evaluation of, the developer's models; which agreements count is in `dataset.method`. `epoch_model` shows the developer has a frontier model.
- `notes`, with optional `source_ids` for the notes' own evidence (they require `notes`); `searches`, required when none of the three is recorded.
- Generated, never authored (`materialise_facts` derives them; the strict authored contract forbids them): `value` and `latest_event`. `latest_event` is the latest event date (ISO strings order correctly, a partial date before the fuller dates inside it). `value` is `recent_access` when that date certainly falls within the twelve months up to `as_of` (a partial date counts from its first possible day; the boundary day is included; 29 February maps to 28 February), `earlier_access` for an older event, else `by_law` with a legal power, else `access_agreed` with a developer agreement, else `none_found`. Its label comes from the vocabulary, with `<year>` replaced by the year of the latest event (`access_label`).

`searches` items are `{date, query, language, sources_read}`, with a real calendar date. Events and agreements have permanent `id`s (`ID_COLLECTIONS`). Removing a value leaves its bindings stale, and the build rejects them.

Evidence: each exact leaf needs a binding, never its parent object: a mode's `code`, a planned `state`; an event's `model`, `developer`, `date`, `kind` and `epoch_model`; the legal power's `provision` and `conditions`; an agreement's `agreement_id` and `epoch_model`. Attribution guard on each mode, planned state, event `kind`, legal power `provision` and agreement `agreement_id`: at least one bound excerpt names the institute by a recorded name (name, acronym, short, local and former names, acronyms in brackets), or comes from a source whose publisher is one of them. A name another institute shares (AISI, CAISI, AI Security Institute) counts only in a source whose `origin` is the institute's jurisdiction, and a mention inside another institute's longer name never counts. `named_as` records another name the excerpt uses, under the same two limits; it is never the host, the reporting-line body or a national partner. The guard is mechanical; review judges whether the institute is the subject. Every institute carries both objects: the schema requires them and `FLAGS_REQUIRED` makes the build check it.

Generated outputs: per-record `citations` as everywhere; `sources.csv` column `legal_text`; `institutes.csv` columns `testing_capacity` (codes joined with '; ', or the state), `testing_capacity_source_ids`, `testing_capacity_notes`, `testing_capacity_searches`, `model_access` (the generated value), `model_access_latest_event`, `model_access_events`, `model_access_legal_power`, `model_access_agreement_ids`, `model_access_source_ids`, `model_access_notes` and `model_access_searches`. Tests: `tests/test_flags.py`, each gate with its mutation.

## Staffing model

One institute object, `staffing_model`, required on every institute, in the downloads and the API only, beside the dated headcounts in `staff`. The page does not show it and its sources stay off the card's source list (`DOWNLOAD_ONLY_FIELDS` in `scripts/display.py`), so a source cited only by it is not added to the institute's own `source_ids`. The rule is in `dataset.method` (Staffing model); codes, labels and definitions are in `dataset.vocabularies.staffing_model`. Checks are in `scripts/flag_checks.py`.

`staffing_model` is `{code, source_ids, named_as?, notes?, searches?}`. `code` is one of `own_staff`, `member_bodies`, `core_with_partners`, `host_unit`, `planned` or `not_published`. Every code except `not_published` needs `source_ids` and forbids `searches`; `not_published` needs `searches` (the same `{date, query, language, sources_read}` items as the testing and access logs), forbids `named_as`, and may carry `source_ids` only for its `notes`.

Evidence: `staffing_model/code` needs a bound excerpt unless the code is `not_published`. The attribution guard and `named_as` limits are those of testing capacity: a bound excerpt names the institute by a recorded name or comes from its own publication. Relevance cutoff: no bound excerpt may come from a source dated before 2023: its `published` date, or for an undated source its `accessed` date. `member_bodies` requires `legal_form: federation_of_existing_bodies`, as the partner relation `member` does. Every institute carries the field: the schema requires it and `STAFFING_REQUIRED` makes the build check it.

Generated outputs: per-record `citations`; `institutes.csv` columns `staffing_model`, `staffing_model_source_ids`, `staffing_model_notes` and `staffing_model_searches`. Tests: `tests/test_staffing_model.py`, each gate with its mutation.

## Known activity

The qualification policy is in `dataset.method`, with the field contract in the output schema. `qualifies_as` and a non-empty `qualification_note` are required for every output, including exclusions. `type` describes the artefact and `attribution` describes institutional credit; neither is a substitute for the reviewed qualification. The broad publication catalogue and its `first_output` milestone are unchanged and can begin before Known activity.

`output_activity_kind` in `scripts/published.py` reads the explicit decision. `known_activity` selects one item per qualifying kind using `credit.date`. Existing credit-date checks compare at the shared precision and reject dates wholly before the lineage publication date. A credit date of `2025` overlaps a lineage date of `2025-06`, so it is accepted at year precision and sorts ahead of month-precision items. Within each kind, order by the unpadded date string, then lexical `lineage`; lineage is already unique within an institute. Order the selected clauses by date, then lexical kind (`joint_testing` before `published_work` for an equal date). A year such as `2025` precedes `2025-03`. This is deterministic presentation of overlapping partial dates, not proof of their chronological order. Precision is never padded or invented. Equal-date items are not merged: the selected item's citations remain attached to it.

`publish` adds `institutes[].operating` to the downloaded JSON as an array of `{kind, date, lineage, source_ids}`. It does not mutate authored data. `[]` means no qualifying work was found; the state is fully derivable, so there is no stored `confirmed` or `not_confirmed` value or separate assessment date. The download schema accepts this generated array; the authored contract forbids it. Each generated activity row cites the union of excerpt bindings for the selected output’s `credit.institute_id` and `credit.date`. The generated first-output event uses the same credit evidence, including when the earliest catalogue item is excluded from Known activity. An output’s broad `source_ids` list is not a fallback.

`known_activity.csv` mirrors those selected rows, with `institute_id` and `source_urls`. It retains its header and dictionary when empty. `outputs.csv` exports the reviewed qualification and its reason, lineage and attribution, plus `credit_institute_id`, `credit_date` and the compatibility `credited_from` column. `institutes.csv` removes `operating` and `operating_as_of`. The old evidence object and `dataset.operating_rule` have no aliases. Non-output signals have no remaining consumer; people, staff and websites retain their canonical fields, while unique research limits and Germany's BSI website citation remain on the institute.

The page consumes the generated `display.operating` object: `since`, `first_lineage` and ordered `items`; it formats dates with `fmtDate`. It shows "Since" the earliest selected date, cited by that item, with the kinds found in date order on a small second line ("Joint testing, published work"); each later kind cites its own item. The per-kind clauses ("Joint testing since 19 Nov 2024; published work since 30 Sep 2025") lead the tooltip and form the view CSV value. The table sorts by the earliest date. Established institutes with an empty array show "None found yet". Non-established institutes retain evidence in downloads but omit the card row and show a structural dash in the table, with "Not applicable" for screen readers and the view CSV. A missing generated field is a defensive "Not published" fallback, not an assessment state.

[Selection and export tests](../../tests/test_known_activity.py) use hand-worked expectations, real regressions and mutations of primary inputs and forbidden authored fields. [Display tests](../../tests/test_operating_display.py) execute page helpers through Node and deliberately break HTML text and citations. The [rendered-page oracle](../../scripts/check_page.py) selects from the published output catalogue, independently of build and page code. Chrome checks remain required before publication.

## International network

`dataset.network_milestones` is a list of `{id, body, type, date, description, short, source_ids}`. Body is a code in `membership_body`; type is `founding`, `convening`, `joint_testing` or `renamed`. These records describe the network, not an institute or a national status. They do not create memberships or Known activity automatically. IDs are unique, and each record has its own citations.

## Timeline and status

Status and `status_since` are generated from each jurisdiction’s primary `timeline` acts (`timeline_status` in `scripts/published.py`). They are not authored copies. Event codes and their definitions are in `dataset.vocabularies.timeline_event`. The status each code sets:

- `exploration_started`: exploring. `proposed`: proposed. `commitment`: planned. `designated`: established.
- `launched` and `first_output`: milestones with no status effect.
- `withdrawn` removes the event named in `withdraws`, and status is recomputed from the surviving events; `closed` returns the status to none_identified unless a later event applies.

The status is the strongest surviving status-setting event. `status_since` is the date of the earliest event in the current uninterrupted run at that status: a reaffirmation does not reset it, a move from planned to established does. `instrument` records the underlying act, not the channel that reported it: a cabinet decision reported in a press release is a `decision`. An event's `date` is the act's effective date, at the precision the source gives (`YYYY`, `YYYY-MM` or `YYYY-MM-DD`), never padded; events are ordered by date, then by list order for equal or overlapping partial dates. `institutes[].established` is generated from the first unwithdrawn `designated` event; closure preserves that historical establishment date. `institutes[].announced` is generated from the first commitment or proposal event in timeline order, including withdrawn events (`announcement` in `scripts/published.py`, the same reading that starts the intervals), and is null when none is recorded: France, Malaysia and Singapore, designated without a recorded commitment or proposal, have null. The pipeline does not infer an announcement from a designation. Evidence for the date binds to the event's `date`. `institutes[].intervals` is generated from the same timeline and the output catalogue: signed day ranges between announcement, establishment and the first qualifying activity, with the activity intervals null unless the institute is established; `display.intervals` holds the card wording for exact intervals only. The rule is `dataset.method` (Intervals between milestones). Name dates come from the timeline (see [Outputs, names and funding](#outputs-names-and-funding)); network membership and appointments stay in `memberships` and `people`, not in the timeline.

## Exports and checks

Every CSV row exports a nonempty stable `id`. Funding components retain their permanent IDs in JSON and the CSV `sum_of` column. Natural-key aliases use the [stable selector keys](#source-excerpts): output lineage, activity kind, exchange-rate currency and jurisdiction identity for the singleton closest candidate. Parent IDs scope nested identities. Source-excerpt IDs identify quotation text within a source using SHA-256 of the exact text. Duplicate quotations can share this identity; their bindings and copy URLs remain separate rows. Binding, copy-URL and order changes retain quote identity; quotation edits change its content hash. Owner reports use these identities and keep snapshot pointers separate where needed. The base comparison matches records by permanent IDs or their documented natural keys before reporting changed leaves at their snapshot locations.

`dist/data/events.csv` merges dated jurisdiction timeline events, people appointments, memberships, agreements, funding decisions and network milestones. It includes jurisdiction and institute IDs and names, type, date, description, short, source IDs and URLs, `origin_kind`, `origin_id`, `source_pointer`, `withdraws` and `scheduled`. A withdrawal links to the earlier event's `origin_id`; historical rows remain visible. Network events have empty jurisdiction/institute fields; memberships belong to jurisdictions. Rows without an actual date are omitted. Funding uses only `decision_date`, never `period`. Names are not rows: each name change appears once, as the timeline event the name links to (`institutes[].names[].event`). Canonical records are emitted once; distinct acts on one date are retained, not collapsed merely because dates match. Outputs remain a separate catalogue.

Event `origin_id` is scoped by its parent: `jurisdiction_id:event_id` for timeline rows, `jurisdiction_id:body` for memberships, and `institute_id:record_id` for appointments, agreements and funding decisions. Network milestones use their permanent record ID. The exported row `id` combines `origin_kind:origin_id`, so the same local record ID under different parents remains distinct. `source_pointer` identifies the originating record's location in this published JSON snapshot.

Sort by the unpadded ISO date string, then origin kind and stable row `id`. A year sorts before a month within that year. This is deterministic presentation, not a claim to know the order of overlapping partial dates. `scheduled` is true when the date is wholly after `dataset.as_of`; future events are not silently presented as completed acts. The datapackage describes all event columns. `outputs.csv`, `network_milestones.csv`, `source_excerpts.csv` and `related_bodies.csv` expose the new nested data; missing source language exports as empty, not English.

Tests cover the migration, nested citations, bad/escaped/unrelated pointers, missing glosses, invalid dates, unchanged status derivation, event origins, partial dates, undated exclusions, future flags and the CSV/datapackage contract. Before each commit, run `uv run scripts/check.py` (see [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md)).

## Revised research method

The editorial rules themselves are in `dataset.method`, which ships with the data and wins over any wording here. This section records the fields that represent those rules and what the build enforces.

For the broad publication catalogue and first-output milestone, outputs need a `lineage` key and `attribution` (`own_publication`, `institutional_author` or `named_contributor`), with an optional `attribution_note`. Each lineage has one item, its latest title and earliest public date. The first-output event is generated from the earliest institute-credit date across the whole catalogue. Its date and credit citations come from that output, and its standard description uses the output title.

Agreements carry `participation` (`institute_party`, `named_implementer` or `recipient`) and `domestic_interagency: false`. The direct-party code means the source names the institute as a party to an agreement. A named implementer carries out a government agreement; a recipient is the named beneficiary of a company pledge, including a documented predecessor. Only `pledged` records use `recipient`, and every pledge requires it. The agreement CSV exports `participation` with these definitions in the data dictionary. `state` distinguishes `signed`, `announced` and `pledged`. What each state and participation code requires is in `dataset.method` (Agreements). Domestic inter-agency agreements are excluded (`domestic_interagency: false`). An `institute` counterparty must identify its peer record in `peer_institute_id`; research organisations outside the institute roster use `other`.

Which amounts need an annual figure is set by `dataset.method` (Annual funding). Published annual figures carry `annual_amount`, `annual_basis` and `annual_basis_code`: `allocation_current_year`, `period_mean` or `other`. Current allocations have inclusive `annual_period` dates. Where published, `annual_profile` contains dated fiscal allocations, amounts and sources. The producer derives means and current-profile figures as described under [Generated funding values](#generated-funding-values). Other annual figures remain authored, with their limits in `annual_basis`; an annual basis requires an annual amount unless the producer calculates it from those recorded inputs.

Negative checks retain actual `searches` with date, language, query, `national_or_official` and source IDs. The confidence each record can reach is set by `dataset.method` (Status confidence); the build enforces it through these fields. `language_note` records selected languages and limits for multilingual states. `governance_source_ids` selects the primary governance evidence the method asks for from the record's citations; `governance_note` states what it shows and its limits. Both fields must occur together, including on low-confidence records. The build checks primary type and citation membership, while relevance remains an editorial judgement. Historical `independent_source_ids` and `independence_note` remain audit metadata and no longer affect the confidence limit. Original query dates and full-check dates remain visible. For high, the build checks `explicit_absence` with `source_id` and exact publication `date`, plus that source's excerpt of the statement; the source must be primary and dated between the inclusive calendar anniversary one year before `dataset.as_of` and `dataset.as_of`. On 29 February the anniversary is 28 February. Older statements permit at most medium confidence. The build reports an error rather than silently changing the record.

`check.last_checked` is the last full re-check, not the most recent edit. `check.partial` lists `{date, scope}` for source, identifier, excerpt and other partial work. Neither check date may be after `dataset.as_of`. Where only the earlier recorded date survives, `last_checked_basis: historical_upper_bound` makes that limit explicit. It bounds the claimed check date, not guaranteed freshness or proof that a full check occurred; `recorded_full_check` denotes a documented full check. What advances each date is set by `dataset.method` (Freshness).

AI Act authority records carry `designation_basis`: `law`, `decree`, `government_decision`, `cabinet_decision`, `ministerial_act` or `none_yet`. Designated bodies need a basis in force. Where no national designation act is identified, use `none_yet`. Precedence between national acts and the Commission list is set by `dataset.method` (AI Act authorities); `designation_note` records differences and interim roles. All EU/EEA authority records also cite `eu-ai-act-authorities-2026`.

Required excerpts point to primary leaves: the decisive timeline act and date, designation date where used, each output’s institutional credit and credit date, legal-basis type and state, stated and annual budget amounts, staff total/technical/target figures, and each agreement’s counterparty, state, date, scope and participation where present. Parent pointers do not satisfy this coverage. Top-level fields inherit their record's citation list; that proves only a provenance link. Derived annual means need excerpts for the stated total and period, not a pretended quotation of the calculation. Repeated identical glosses on different excerpts within one source fail validation; a gloss translates only its excerpt.

People carry `title_status` (`published` or `unpublished`); the title rule is in `dataset.method` (Leadership), and the build checks that unpublished titles use role `lead` with the note `no formal title published`.

The revised completeness checks run unconditionally through `check_method_rules`. Every present `legal_basis` requires exact supporting pointers for both `type` and `state`, including `type: none_found`. Established institutes require `legal_basis`, but planned, proposed and exploring records may omit an unknown future constitutive act. All positive records still require `mandate_short` and `placement`. Published `annual_profile` amounts also require exact quote pointers, including when the displayed annual amount is a derived period mean. USD annual estimates are calculated, so quotations cover their stated-currency inputs and the exchange-rate table instead of the results. Wikidata identifiers are unique across jurisdictions and institutes together. Excerpt pointers must identify an individual related body rather than the `related_bodies` list, and that body must cite the source itself.

The one-institute-per-jurisdiction relation remains enforced by the reciprocal `institute_id` and `jurisdiction_id` links. The EU AI Office is an institute record and can be a peer counterparty. Agreements with several parties use separate bilateral records where supported, or a non-peer `multilateral` counterparty.

The new evidence metadata are available in canonical JSON. The funding CSV includes `annual_basis_code`; comparison metadata added later is documented below. Required exact leaves are checked unconditionally by `check_method_rules`. The explicit-absence object has separate coverage: its own official source excerpt supports the statement as a whole, while its publication date is checked separately. Governance evidence and language coverage are reviewed editorially; the build checks recorded queries, cited primary source IDs and notes, not whether a source proves absence.

## Short event text

Every current timeline event and network milestone has `short`: a plain, single-line summary drawn only from its description and sources. The build requires it on every event of a positive-status jurisdiction, including exploring, and on every network milestone. Any short present on a negative jurisdiction's timeline is also validated. It must be nonblank, have no surrounding whitespace or line separators, and contain at most 12 words counted with Python `str.split()`; hyphenated terms count as one word. The schema defines the string field; the build enforces the length and completeness rules.

`timeline.csv`, `network_milestones.csv` and `events.csv` put `short` immediately after `description`, including in empty-file headers and the data dictionary. Other event origins leave this column empty. Full descriptions, dates, citations and source pointers remain canonical and unchanged; short text does not add facts or replace evidence.

## Authority and freshness checks

Every related body requires `kind: ai_act_authority | other`. An authority requires its own sources, `counts_as_aisi: false`, `designation_basis` and optional `designation_state`; EU/EEA authorities also cite the Commission list. Authority metadata on `kind: other` is rejected. Classification is independent of the optional role codes, so deleting designation metadata cannot bypass the checks. The related-body CSV exports the classification. Jurisdiction and institute CSVs export `last_checked_basis` beside the recorded date, retaining the distinction between a documented full check and a historical upper bound.

Every changelog entry requires a plain-language `summary` for readers. Historical summaries describe the changes already recorded for that version; they do not revise historical data.

Output `date` is the earliest public date of the shared document lineage. `credit.date` records the first public version with qualifying institutional credit; it requires its own excerpt alongside the institutional identity binding. What establishes credit is in `dataset.method` (Outputs, First output). The output CSV exports the credit identity and date alongside the generated compatibility field; see [Known activity](#known-activity).

Output `lineage` keys are global document identifiers across institute catalogues; the same key must retain the same publication date everywhere.

Every source has `origin`: an ISO jurisdiction code, EU, a named body from `dataset.vocabularies.source_origin`, or null if unresolved. The source CSV retains it. The build rejects unknown codes but does not fail merely because a claim cites foreign evidence; `build.py --report-dir DIR` writes an informational report to that directory; without the flag nothing is written.

## Source-title translations

Source titles retain their recorded wording in `title`; optional `title_en` holds the English translation that `dataset.method` (Source titles) calls for. The source's `language` describes the document read, not its title. `sources.csv` exports both fields, with an empty `title_en` cell when omitted.

## Advisory reports

Timeline `instrument` gains `advisory_report` for a government-appointed advisory commission's report. The page labels a status set by such a report as an advisory recommendation.

## Closest candidate

Optional `jurisdictions[].closest_body` records one body for a `none_identified` jurisdiction: `{name, stage, gap, note, source_ids}`. The inclusion rule and mutually exclusive gaps are in `dataset.method`; stage and gap codes are described in the schema and exported data dictionary. Omission means no body was selected, not that no related body exists. The field does not confer institute status and does not replace the broader `related_bodies` list.

`closest_bodies.csv` exports one row per selected body, with jurisdiction ID, name, stage, gap, note and citation IDs/URLs. It uses the existing comma-separated file format and semicolon-separated citation lists. The file and dictionary are emitted even when there are no rows. The schema restricts the field to negative jurisdictions and rejects lists, missing fields and unknown codes. The build checks source references and exact excerpt pointers for name, stage and note. Quotes support the factual basis of the note; the gap and absence of an assignment remain editorial judgements. Each body has its own citations, which need not also occur on its parent jurisdiction.

The timeline code `related_body_planned` records an official plan for a non-qualifying related body, with no jurisdiction-status effect. Panama's retained strategy adoption uses this code; its status reclassification is an editorial change recorded in the changelog, not a government withdrawal.

## Generated funding values

The producer derives these fields before currency conversion and display selection:

| Authored funding record | Generated fields | Primary inputs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sum_of` | `amount`, `annual_amount` | The corresponding values on the named component records, derived first if necessary |
| `annual_basis_code: period_mean` | `annual_amount` | `amount / years`, with a positive number of years |
| `annual_basis_code: allocation_current_year` with a non-empty `annual_profile` | `annual_amount`, `annual_period` | The profile row whose inclusive `start` and `end` contain `dataset.as_of` |

Strict authoring rejects these generated fields on the corresponding records. Component references must resolve uniquely within the institute; self-reference, duplicate components and cycles fail. A current-profile record must have exactly one matching row, so a gap or overlap fails rather than selecting a convenient value. A current-allocation record without a profile retains its directly sourced annual amount and period. Primary inputs retain their excerpt bindings; generated values receive citations from those inputs.

`eur_per_year_est` is generated in published JSON and CSV, absent from authored data. The single rule is `annual_eur_estimate` in `scripts/published.py`: multiply `annual_amount` by the currency's `eur_per_unit` (EUR uses 1), then round to six significant figures using decimal arithmetic: up for an upper-bound `amount_qualifier`, down for a lower bound, otherwise half up, so a bound stays true (`bound_rounding`, shared with the budget ratios). Zero stays zero and an unknown annual amount produces null. `funding.csv` also carries `units_per_eur`, the rate used. Quotations cover the primary stated-currency inputs, period and exchange-rate inputs. There is no updater script or stored result to synchronise. Period means use unrounded division in the original currency. The page shows budget and staff figures at two significant figures through the one `round2` rule in `scripts/display.py`; a stated lower bound rounds down and an upper bound up, so a bound stays true. The counts row's median and highest figures are generated in `dataset.display.counts` by `summary_figures`.

`eur_per_person_est`, `share_of_gdp_est` and `ratio_denominators` are generated the same way, on the compared funding record of a state only (`display.comparison_funding`; the EU, territories and other records get none), and are forbidden in authored data. `budget_ratios` in `scripts/published.py` owns the arithmetic and `comparable_funding`, also there, the selection, so `display.py` imports both and no layer recomputes them. Inputs are unrounded: the annual amount (`exact_annual_amount`: a period mean is recomputed as amount over years, never read from its float export), the `eur_per_unit` rates, `population.value` and `gdp.value` (World Bank WDI GDP in current national currency, recorded on `jurisdictions[].gdp` with its year and source). The share of GDP is taken in the budget currency when it is the GDP currency, so no exchange rate enters; otherwise both are converted to EUR. Results have six significant figures, rounded the way `round2` rounds a bound. `amount_qualifier` applies to them unchanged. A missing or non-positive denominator, or a GDP currency without a rate, gives null. Their citations join the compared figure's with the denominator's; `gdp` and the ratios are download-only fields (`DOWNLOAD_ONLY_FIELDS`), so their sources stay off the page's source lists. `funding.csv` carries both ratios with the year and source ids of the denominator behind each; `jurisdictions.csv` carries `gdp`. `display.per_person` is the two-figure label for the page. The editorial rule is `budget-ratios` in `dataset.method`.

The conversion covers all funding kinds, including nominal supplementary allocations for a stated year, without changing their scope or commitment labels. Chart selection lives in `comparable_funding` in `scripts/published.py`, tested against hand-worked cases; it implements the comparison rule in `dataset.method` (Annual funding). A total names stable component IDs in authored and published `sum_of`; the producer calculates both totals, and validation checks the source union, common period, currency, commitment and lowest component confidence. Inclusive `annual_period` dates avoid parsing prose or inferring fiscal calendars. These fields are part of the initial public schema.

Funding CSV exports `coverage`, `derived_total` and inclusive `annual_period_start`/`annual_period_end`, so components and their sum remain distinguishable. `coverage: whole_institute` identifies an institute-directed allocation; it does not assert that the amount includes all institute resources. Component quotations cover the calculation inputs; the build checks the derived total. Qualified components cannot be summed: the current contract has no general rule for combining strict, inclusive or approximate bounds. Annual periods require full dates; the shared full-date schema now checks calendar validity as well as spelling. Decision dates retain their recorded year, month or day precision.

Period means are labelled as averages over the stated period. Whether component scopes are disjoint remains an evidence judgement.


## Field citations and rendering

Every cited record exports `citations`, a mapping from field name to source IDs derived from the excerpts supporting that field. Derived fields follow their primary inputs: status follows the decisive act, EUR figures follow amounts and rates, and activity follows institutional credit. Missing bindings produce an empty citation list; broad source association never substitutes for an excerpt. Authored `source_ids` remains the full research-source association, including contextual material; generated activity and first-output source lists instead contain their credit evidence. The producer includes those associations in `display.source_links` for the full catalogue list.

Generated institute `display` fields carry selected funding and staff IDs, `funding_records` and `staff_records` maps keyed by those IDs, and explicit `funding_ids`, `staff_ids` and `related_funding_ids` display orders. Selection fields are `comparison_funding`, `budget`, `staff`, `total_staff` and `technical_staff`; absent selections are null. Funding eligibility and selection policy live in [`dataset.method`](../../data/aisi.json) (Annual funding and Budget display). Object-key order in the record maps has no display meaning.

For Budget fallback ranking and `funding_ids`, the date key is `decision_date` when present, otherwise `annual_period.end`, otherwise an empty string. Sort that key descending, then funding ID lexically ascending; undated records therefore follow dated ones. `related_funding_ids` retains the relative order of its members in `funding_ids`. Staff selection and `staff_ids` use descending recorded `as_of`, then lexical staff ID; selection searches for a total, then a technical count, then the latest remaining record. `display.operating.items` uses ascending date, kind and lineage; `since` and `first_lineage` identify the first item. Recent-development groups use descending date, then ascending jurisdiction ID and lexical event ID; same-day `events` and corresponding `texts` use lexical event ID, while partial dates remain separate. These deterministic orders do not assert chronology within overlapping partial dates. No choice depends on the authored record-array position.

Each funding record's `display` contains `annual_estimate`, a compact `value`, a `qualifiers` list and `budget_citations`, plus `native_value` and `native_qualifiers` for the primary-currency record. The value preserves `amount_qualifier`, including strict and inclusive bounds; annual conversions remain approximate. Period, kind and commitment appear separately from the amount. Notes stay out of the compact amount and appear, cited, in the funding disclosure. Funding disclosures show the native amount and structured qualifiers. Notes and confidence remain visible; full descriptions appear unchanged apart from currency normalisation in a nested Source statement disclosure when they add text beyond the native amount. Records without an amount keep their description visible. Native amounts cite amount, currency, qualifier, period, commitment, kind and coverage; source statements cite description; notes and confidence retain their own field citations. No period or commitment is appended to a source statement. The separate Related funding line reads `related_funding_ids`.

Jurisdiction and institute `display.source_links`, field citation lists and `budget_citations` follow the source-catalogue order, with duplicate IDs removed. Dataset `display.maintainers` sorts name then affiliation lexically ascending; `display.changelog` sorts date then numeric version descending, and `latest_change` is its first entry or null. Each other-overview entry exports `display.source_links` in that entry’s authored `source_ids` order; those links are ordered list content rather than claim citations. Funding `qualifiers` follow funding kind or annual unit, period-mean label, period, commitment and confidence, omitting inapplicable entries; `native_qualifiers` uses funding kind, period and commitment, with notes and confidence rendered separately. The page reads these generated selections, maps and ordered lists; it does not inspect raw funding, staff, timeline or source-ID arrays. [Render boundary tests](../../tests/test_render_only.py) reject raw-array reads, and [display tests](../../tests/test_display.py) reverse and shuffle source record collections. The browser oracle independently checks permanent funding IDs, including Japan's latest own allocation. The owner must run the browser gate and screenshot comparison before integration.
